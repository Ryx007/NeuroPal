3. Images from correlations

Send one photon of each pair through an object and onto a detector that cannot see where it lands. Send its twin, untouched, onto a camera. Neither detector shows the object. Yet sorting the camera's clicks by whether the first detector fired at the same moment draws a picture of something the camera never saw.

This is ghost imaging. It works because the pair shares its geometry: the position of one photon on the camera tells you where its twin crossed the object. Correlation plenoptic imaging takes the same idea further, recording directions as well as positions, so the picture can be refocused after the measurement.
