1. Light, counted one photon at a time

A detector that clicks does not tell you what light is. It tells you that, at one instant and one place, some energy was absorbed. Everything interesting about quantum optics hides in how those clicks are arranged in time.

Put two detectors behind a beam splitter and count the moments when both click together. For thermal light the coincidences come more often than chance would allow; the photons arrive bunched. For a single-photon source they almost never come together. The ratio between the two rates is written g(2)(0), and it is one of the cleanest experimental fingerprints of non-classical light.

Try this: before reading on, predict what g(2)(0) is for a laser well above threshold.

For coherent light the clicks are independent, so g(2)(0) = 1. Thermal light gives 2. A perfect single-photon source gives 0, a value no classical field can reach.
